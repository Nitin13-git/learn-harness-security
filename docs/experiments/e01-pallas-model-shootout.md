tasks, labeled 021, 005, 033, 016, 028), independently verified — a flag

## Reading it through this course's lens

**Turn count tracks something real, and it isn't just "thoroughness."**
`sonnet-4.5` solved 033 in 273 turns — more than double the next-highest
solver (`kimi-k2.5`, 110 turns) — and timed out on all four other tasks.
`minimax-m2.5` and `glm-5` both solved two tasks each in under 110 turns.
That's not evidence that more turns is bad; it's evidence that *this
particular benchmark design* rewards models that converge fast more than it
rewards models that eventually get there. A harness's turn/timeout budget is
itself a scope decision (L02) — set it wrong and you're measuring budget
tolerance, not capability.

**Zero solves is not the same failure mode every time.** `devstral-2-123b`
mostly `miss`es — it ran to completion without finding the vulnerability.
`gpt-oss-120b` mostly `incompl`s — something about its run didn't complete
the methodology at all. `deepseek-v3.2` has two `invalid`s — the engine
*never drove* those two attempts. Collapsing all three into "0 solves"
in a leaderboard throws away exactly the triage information L03 argues for:
you cannot tell "the model tried and failed" from "the harness never gave it
a fair shot" without keeping the failure category, not just the verdict.

**No benchmark is universally solved, and that's useful information too.**
021, 016, and 033 were each solved by at least one model; 005 and 028 were
not solved by anyone in this run. A benchmark nobody solves is either
genuinely harder, or has a methodology bug shared by every attempt — the
only way to tell the difference is to look at *why* every attempt on 005
and 028 failed, the same triage discipline from L03 applied across models
instead of within one.

**This is exactly the kind of data EvoBench exists to use.** Every `miss`
and `t/o` row above is a candidate regression task: a specific model, on a
specific benchmark, with a specific recorded failure mode. See
[E02](e02-evobench-end-to-end.md) for what happens when one of those
failures gets mined instead of just logged.
